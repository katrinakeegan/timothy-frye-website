I study the politics of autocracy and post-Soviet countries, especially Russia. My recent research focuses on public opinion, elections, and the Ukraine war. I have also written extensively on economic reform, property rights, and firm behavior in Russia and other post-communist countries. My most recent book, [_Workplace Politics: How Politicians and Employers Subvert Elections_]({{ '/books/#workplace-politics' | relative_url }}), coauthored with Ora John Reuter and David Szakonyi, examines the incentives and costs for firms and government officials to mobilize voters in the workplace in eight countries, with a particular focus on Russia. My research has been published in the _American Political Science Review_, _World Politics_, the _Journal of Politics_, _Comparative Political Studies_, and many other journals. I was also the editor of the journal _Post-Soviet Affairs_ from 2016-2026.

My work spans audiences inside and outside academia, such as my book [_Weak Strongman: The Limits of Power in Putin's Russia_]({{ '/books/#weak-strongman' | relative_url }}) and my articles in _Foreign Affairs_ and [other general interest publications]({{ '/commentary/' | relative_url }}). I serve on the Library of Congress Scholars Council and the Kennan Institute Advisory Council. I have previously consulted for the World Bank, USAID, and the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development.

I have been a professor at Columbia since 2006. I teach core graduate and undergraduate surveys of Comparative Politics. I also teach graduate and undergraduate courses on the politics of autocracy. I have previously served as Chair of the Department of Political Science and the Director of the Harriman Institute for Russian, Eurasian and East European Studies. Columbia is my alma mater: I received my PhD from the Department of Political Science and my MA from the School of International and Public Affairs.
