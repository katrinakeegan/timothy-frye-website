Timothy Frye

Marshall D. Shulman Professor of Post-Soviet Foreign Policy
Department of Political Science, Columbia University 420 W. 118th Street, 811 IAB, New York, NY 10027 · tmf2 [at] columbia [dot] edu

I study the politics of autocracy and post-Soviet countries, especially Russia. My recent research focuses on public opinion, elections, and the Ukraine war. I have also written extensively on economic reform, property rights, and firm behavior in Russia and other post-communist countries. My most recent book, Workplace Politics: How Politicians and Employers Subvert Elections, coauthored with Ora John Reuter and David Szakonyi, examines the incentives and costs for firms and government officials to mobilize voters in the workplace in eight countries, with a particular focus on Russia. My research has been published in the American Political Science Review, World Politics, the Journal of Politics, Comparative Political Studies, and many other journals. I was also the editor of the journal Post-Soviet Affairs from 2016-2026.

My work spans audiences inside and outside academia, such as my book Weak Strongman: The Limits of Power in Putin’s Russia and my articles in Foreign Affairs and other general interest publications. I serve on the Library of Congress Scholars Council and the Kennan Institute Advisory Council. I have previously consulted for the World Bank, USAID, and the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development.

I have been a professor at Columbia since 2006. I teach the core graduate and undergraduate surveys of Comparative Politics. I also teach graduate and undergraduate courses on the politics of autocracy. I have previously served as Chair of the Department of Political Science and the Director of the Harriman Institute for Russian, Eurasian and East European Studies. Columbia is my alma mater: I received my PhD from the Department of Political Science and my MA from the School of International and Public Affairs.
